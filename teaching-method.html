Our Teaching Methodology

In the field of manual medicine, once you become experienced enough to start teaching, you can put your finger on a landmark and exert the correct vector of force almost instinctively, without thinking about it. A beginning teacher will often be heard saying “What is wrong with these people?” when a student fumbles a technique that was shown clearly and smoothly. As a teacher gains teaching experience, he comes to the realization that he has lost touch (so to speak) with what a student who is seeing this technique for the first time is experiencing.

Another, and even more difficult to fix, scenario is when a teacher has taught a technique and watched the student perform the technique himself, without prompting. However, when the student goes home and short-term muscle memory has faded, he finds he cannot do the technique the way he did it in the class. The student will conclude that he is not sufficiently capable, the technique is too difficult, or that the technique is phony.

The method of teaching presented here addresses these issues: Landmarks: There are step-by-step instructions, all with accompanying pictures, for finding critical landmarks. This includes a series of progressively more difficult exercises for finding these landmarks, culminating in how to find the landmarks in the treatment position. Diagnosis and Treatment: For the actual diagnosis and treatment techniques, we present the movement of the underlying bone, and then a series of progressively more difficult exercises in doing these techniques, culminating in how to do the technique in a clinical treatment scenario.

Finally, for areas where there are a lot of interactions, we describe a framework for keeping a logical thought process as you progress through the diagnosis and treatment.
